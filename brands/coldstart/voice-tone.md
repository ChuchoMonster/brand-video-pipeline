## Voice profile

**Confident, terse, declarative.** ColdStart writes the way a senior consultant talks — short statements, no hedging, no qualifiers. The sentences are short enough to read in one breath. There's no "we believe" or "we think" softening. The position is asserted as fact.

## Recurring patterns

1. **Affirmation framing**: "Yes. X can do that." — leads with the answer, then names what. Used to short-circuit doubt.
2. **Direct address**: "Your team is doing X manually..." — second person. Names the reader's situation directly.
3. **Quantified claims**: "100x Faster." Numerical assertions stand alone as headlines, not hidden in body copy.
4. **Pointed urgency without alarmism**: surfaces the cost of inaction ("manually what AI can do in seconds") without tipping into FUD.
5. **Punctuation as rhythm**: full stops mid-line ("Yes. Claude Can Do That.") create staccato that reads like spoken delivery.

## Anti-patterns (don't sound like this)

- Corporate softening: "We can help you explore how AI might..." — never. ColdStart asserts.
- Buzzword stacking: "leverage cutting-edge AI to synergize..." — never. Plain words.
- Question hooks: "Are you tired of...?" — not the voice. ColdStart tells, doesn't ask.
- Long compound sentences with three clauses — not the rhythm.
- Hedged claims: "up to 100x faster in some cases" — neither hedge nor footnote. State it.

## Audience

B2B sales / marketing / RevOps teams. Decision-makers (founders, VPs, ops leads) who already suspect AI applies to their workflows but haven't shipped it. Not technical buyers — they care about outcomes (faster, cheaper, more leads), not implementation detail.

## Positioning

ColdStart is the **bridge between B2B teams and AI capability** — specifically Claude implementation. The headline "Yes. Claude Can Do That." positions the company as the affirmative answer to "can AI do [my workflow]?". Not a generic AI agency — Claude-specialist.

## Apply this to scripts (Stage 3 in the pipeline)

When the script generator produces explainer-video copy for ColdStart, it must:

- **Open with an affirmation** ("Yes. ___.") or a declarative claim ("ColdStart shipped ___ in ___ days.") — never a question hook.
- **Use second person** ("Your team", "Your CRM", "Your pipeline") to name the viewer's situation.
- **Quantify** at least one claim per video (time saved, leads generated, cost cut). Whole numbers and round multipliers (10x, 100x) read better than "37.5%".
- **Keep sentences under 12 words on average.** Punchy beats elaborate.
- **End with a soft CTA** matching "Start a Conversation" — consultative, not "Buy now."
- **Never apologize for AI** — no "we know AI feels overwhelming" preambles. The audience already wants this; ColdStart's job is to remove friction, not address skepticism.
